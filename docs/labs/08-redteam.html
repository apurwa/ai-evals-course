      Fourteen attacks were refused. One was permitted, and it is the one that does not try to
      exceed anyone's authority: it shortchanges the customer by $150 while staying comfortably
      inside every limit the gate enforces.
    

    

      That is not an oversight in the implementation. Every rule in this gate is about the
      company's exposure, and an under-refund creates none. The gate is doing exactly what it
      was built to do. It was simply never built to protect the customer.
    

    

      This is worth sitting with, because it is the shape of most real authorization layers.
      They encode the risks somebody priced. Whether the other party needs protection too is a
      product question, and it will not be raised by a security review that only asks whether
      attacks were blocked. The check that catches this one is not a permission rule at all: it
      is an eval, comparing what was paid against what rules.py says was owed.
