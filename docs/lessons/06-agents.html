Lesson 6/Module 3/~55 min

Evaluating Agents

Score the path, not just the destination, and measure whether the agent does the right thing every time rather than some time.

01 Objective

You can evaluate a multi-step agent at the trajectory level, and you can tell the difference between a system that is capable and a system that is reliable.

02 The failure this prevents

Reporting pass@k and calling it quality. If you run each scenario five times and count a scenario as passing when any run passed, you have measured whether the agent can do the task. Your customer gets one attempt. They experience pass^k, the probability it works every time, and those two numbers diverge violently as soon as the agent is even slightly unreliable.

At 90 percent per-step success over five steps, the agent succeeds end to end 59 percent of the time. Nothing in a per-step dashboard shows you that number, and per-step is what most teams instrument.

03 Concepts
Trajectory evaluation
Scoring the sequence of steps against what the sequence should have been. Not string equality: there are usually several acceptable orders, and some steps are mandatory while others are optional.
Required, forbidden, optional
The three categories that make trajectory scoring tractable. For a return: check_return_eligibility is required, an air label on hazmat is forbidden, search_orders is optional. Score against those, not against a golden transcript.
pass@k, capability
The probability at least one of k attempts succeeds. Useful for research, and for deciding whether a task is achievable at all.
pass^k, reliability
The probability all k attempts succeed. This is what a user with one shot experiences, and it is what a product owner should be shown.
Compounding
Multi-step success multiplies. Five steps at 0.95 is 0.77 end to end. This is why removing a step is often a bigger quality win than improving one.
Freeze and read once
Split scenarios 15 train, 40 dev, 45 test. Iterate on dev. Touch test at the very end, once. A test set you have looked at twice is a dev set that is lying to you.

07 Traps
  • Check that you reported pass^k to anyone making a product decision. pass@k belongs in a research note, not a launch review.
  • Check that your trajectory scorer allows more than one correct order. If it does not, you are testing for a transcript and will fail every acceptable variation.
  • Check that a step budget exhaustion is scored as a failure, not skipped because the trace has no final answer to grade.
  • Check that you have not read the test set. If you have, say so, and treat every number from it as a dev number.
  • Check whether the fastest quality win is removing a step rather than improving one. Compounding usually says yes.
08 Self-check
  • Your agent is 92 percent reliable per step over six steps. What does a customer see?
  • Why is a golden transcript a bad trajectory target, and what replaces it?
  • When is pass@k the number you actually want?
  • You have looked at your test set three times this month. What is it now?
