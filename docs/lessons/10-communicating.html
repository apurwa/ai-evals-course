Lesson 10/Bonus/~45 min

Talking About This Work

Explain an eval suite to someone who will fund it, staff it, or interview you about it.

01 Objective

You can describe what you built, why each decision was made, and what you would do differently, in the language of whoever is asking.

02 The failure this prevents

Doing excellent work nobody funds. Eval infrastructure is invisible when it succeeds: the incident that did not happen generates no story. If you cannot articulate what a regression would have cost, the budget goes to the feature with a demo.

In interviews the failure has a different shape. Candidates describe tools they used rather than decisions they made. "We used LLM as a judge" says almost nothing. "We used a judge for tone and code checks for everything decidable, because a model asked to count tool calls is a slow and occasionally wrong in operator" says you have actually done this.

03 Concepts
Lead with the failure, not the method
"Our agent was refunding customers less than they were owed and every permission check passed" gets attention. "We built an eval harness" does not.
Translate to money or risk
An under-refund at 3 percent of 40,000 monthly returns is a number a finance director can act on. A TPR of 0.86 is not, until you convert it.
Show the decision, not the tool
The interesting content is always why you chose one thing over another and what it cost you. Anyone can list libraries.
Name the limitation before you are asked
Saying "these traces are scripted, so the distribution is not what a real model produces" builds more credibility than any result you could present. It also makes everything else you say more believable.
The one-minute version
System, failure found, how you measured it, what changed, what you would do next. Practise it out loud. It is the answer to at least four different interview questions.

Interview drill and portfolio builder

Twelve questions drawn from what you actually built in this course, with a rubric for what a strong answer contains. Write your answers, save them locally, and assemble a portfolio summary you can put in front of someone.

Open the lab

06 Local lab

Fork this repository, run the whole course on your own machine, and then make it yours: change the world, change the policy, find failures this corpus does not contain. A fork with your own scenarios and your own taxonomy is a far better portfolio piece than a completed tutorial, because the interesting part is the judgment, and judgment only shows when the answers were not provided. 

make check                        # prove it works
python scripts/build_lab_corpus.py
# then change data/world/facts.yaml and see what breaks

Write down every gate that failed and why. That list is the most interesting artifact you will produce, and it is the one nobody else's tutorial fork will have.

07 Traps
  • Check that you led with a failure, not a method.
  • Check that you named a limitation before being asked. It is the fastest way to be believed.
  • Check that your numbers have units somebody cares about. Convert.
  • Check that you can say what you would do differently. No answer here reads as no reflection.
  • Check that you are not claiming a synthetic result as a measured one. It will be found out, and everything else you said goes with it.
08 Self-check
  • Give the one-minute version of what you built, out loud, without notes.
  • Convert your worst failure mode into an annual cost. State your assumptions.
  • What is the weakest part of your eval suite, and what would you do with two more weeks?
  • Explain to a non-technical executive why 94 percent accuracy might mean nothing.
